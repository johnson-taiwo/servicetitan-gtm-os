## The ICP in one sentence

**US and Canadian trade contractors (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, garage door) with 10 to 99 employees, run by
the owner, that are adding technicians or a second location while still running the business on paper, spreadsheets,
QuickBooks or a starter field-service app.**

Why this line and not "any contractor": ServiceTitan prices per field technician (co: pricing; sg §1 #4) and grows
when customers "hire more technicians" and "expand into additional locations" (sg §1 #3). It told investors it does
"not focus on down-market trades businesses, which we define as having five employees or fewer" (co: IPO). Its own
comparison pages aim at shops with "ambitions to grow" beyond Jobber or Housecall Pro (sg §1 #9). So the buyer is the
contractor at the moment of crossing from a shop to a company.

## Layer 3. Intent signals (why now)

| Signal | Why it means "now" | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring technicians, CSRs or dispatchers | Per-technician pricing; growth is the stated trigger | sg §1 #2-#4; sg §2d |
| Opening a second location or branch | "expand into additional locations" | sg §1 #3, #15 |
| Bought by, or bought into, a PE platform | Platforms standardise operations, "in many cases on our platform" | sg §1 #6, #10; mk §5 |
| Storm hits the service area (roofing, restoration) | Demand spike the office can't schedule on paper | sg §2b; pr §5 |
| New permits filed (mechanical, plumbing, electrical) | Job volume and project work rising | sg §2a |
| New or upgraded contractor licence | New company, or a new trade line | sg §2c |
| Review count growing fast | Volume growing faster than the office | sg §2e (INFERRED as a trigger) |

Market pressure that makes these sharper: skilled-trades shortage (technician productivity matters more), the end of
the 25C and 25D home-energy credits on 31 Dec 2025, and the R-410A to A2L refrigerant change (mk §1, §2, §4).

## Layer 4. Persona (who decides)

The owner decides in most shops; a general manager is "a key influencer" in larger ones (sg §1 #5). Detail in
`personas.md`: champion = operations or general manager, economic buyer = owner, influencer = office manager or CSR lead.

## Not a fit

| Who | Why | Source |

## Challenge pass (read as a sceptical Head of Sales at ServiceTitan, 23 Nov 2026)

| Weak spot found | Fix made |
|---|---|
| "Growing" was a feeling, not a filter | Rewrote every segment and signal as something observable: employee band, location count, job posts, permits, licences |
| The 10-employee floor isn't a ServiceTitan number. Its own floor is "four trucks" and it excludes "five employees or fewer" | Kept 10 as a **targeting** floor, marked INFERRED, because four trucks usually means 6 to 10 staff counting the office, and smaller shops drive the price complaints (cu §7). Shops of 6 to 9 with a clear hiring signal can be added later as a test cell |
| Technographic detection is unproven | Marked INFERRED with a Week 2 test, instead of claiming we can see the current tool |
| Size bands from databases would quietly break the 10 to 99 filter | Added "size can't be proven" to Not a fit, and size proof from public files to the system (pr §2; `context/docs/gtm-system.md` step 5) |
| PE platforms were mixed into the core | Split them out as S2, a different buyer and pitch |
