# Competitors - where ServiceTitan wins, loses, and how to respond

*From `context/docs/research/competitors.md` (cmp) and `customers.md` (cu). INFERRED where marked.*

| Competitor | Who they target | How they position against ServiceTitan |
|---|---|---|
| Housecall Pro | "teams of 1 to 100+" (cmp §2) | "scale without the cost or complexity: no long-term commitments, fast onboarding" (cmp §4) |
| Jobber | Small service businesses, "400,000 service pros" (cmp §2) | Positions itself as the simpler alternative (cmp §4) |
| FieldEdge, Successware, Service Fusion | Multi-truck residential HVAC, plumbing, electrical (cmp §2) | Mid-market rivals with similar scope |
| BuildOps, ServiceTrade | Commercial contractors only (cmp §2) | "residential-first tools like ServiceTitan become profit leaks for commercial teams" (cmp §4, BuildOps) |
| AccuLynx, JobNimbus | Roofing (cmp §2) | Roofing-specific CRM |
| Salesforce, SAP, WorkWave | Horizontal or other verticals (cmp §1) | General field-service platforms |

## Where ServiceTitan wins
- Growing shops that have outgrown a starter app: its comparison pages lead with "A Platform That Scales With You as
  You Grow" (cmp §3).
- Breadth on one platform: reporting and job costing ("Superior Job Costing Reports", cmp §3), payments, marketing,
  call centre, AI agents (co).
- PE platforms standardising acquired brands (sg §1 #6, #10).

## Where it loses
- Price and contract terms: the top complaint ("insane premium", "2-year contract", cu §7); small shops leave as "too
  expensive" (sg §1 #16).
- Onboarding effort and training (cu §7).
- Commercial-heavy contractors, where BuildOps attacks its residential roots (cmp §4).

## How to respond (INFERRED)
- Never argue price in cold outreach. Target the size and growth moment where the platform pays back (icp.md), and let
  proof from a similar-size company carry it.
- Address onboarding head on with the customer stories that describe the switch (cu §4).
- For commercial prospects, lead with commercial proof (Interstate AC, Adams Power, cu §2), not residential stories.
